Where it rests

Bass: the lower soft pad is intended to sit on your right thigh; the other pad supports your right forearm.

Cello: the orange outlines show where lower knee pads are still needed. The strap shown is a proposal, not a tested support.

How you would switch

Stop bowing → park the bow beside the chair → turn the instrument with both hands → settle it on your thigh → press the effects pedal once and pluck.

The frames stay open and locked throughout. The endpin must already be stowed. There is a pause in playing.

This is a generic player, not a measured fitting for you. The bow holder and orange knee-pad outlines are proposed additions, not finished parts. Plain-language notes and remaining work.

The core-to-metal attachment is the open structural interface. The old view omitted the wing connections entirely; this revision shows their proposed geometry.

How seated bass is different now

  • A broad, curved thigh cradle replaces the narrow lower rail.
  • A separate forearm pad rounds the upper contact edge.
  • The neck points upward 20° as an initial fitting position.
  • The old rigid chest rest is removed.

The gray thigh shape is a placement sketch, not a fitted person. Actual thigh contact, balance, hand reach and freedom to bow still need a seated mock-up. A picture cannot establish comfort.

Playing change

Stop bowing at a musical rest, reposition the whole rigid instrument, press the mapped effects footswitch once, then pluck. The frames remain open and locked.

The same four cello strings, curved bridge and fingerboard remain in use. The support/harness and safe bow handoff are still unresolved.

Travel change

Offstage: support the instrument, pull the orange wing locks, fold the frames toward the neck, then reinsert the locks in the stow holes.

Only the ergonomic frames fold. The reinforced musical core stays full length. Lock-pin retainers and tethers are not finalized.
