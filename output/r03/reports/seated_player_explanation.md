The storyboard is an AI-generated concept illustration based on the current CAD views. It is not a photograph, a measured human fitting, or evidence of a working instrument. It does not revise the actual CAD or approve a new support design.

## What the pictures mean

In the intended **bass position**, the lower dark cushion sits on top of the player's right thigh. The upper cushion gives the right forearm a soft contact. The neck points to the player's left and slightly upward. The left hand fingers the strings and the right hand plucks them. The same four cello strings stay in place.

In the intended **upright cello position**, the left hand fingers the neck and the right hand bows. The illustrated strap is only a proposed support. **The current model does not yet establish stable upright support with the endpin stowed.** The dashed orange patches in the close-up show missing lower knee contacts that still need design. They are not existing CAD parts. Do not read the illustration as a claim that the current wing pads fit both poses.

The same padded frames remain open in both playing positions. Nothing folds during the playing change.

## Intended switch, after support has been developed and tested

1. Stop bowing at a rest in the music.
2. Park the bow in a holder beside the chair while the left hand and support retain the instrument. The holder is an additional proposed accessory, not a supplied or tested part.
3. Use both hands to turn the complete rigid instrument toward the bass position, keeping its support attached and its frames locked open.
4. Settle the lower cushion on the right thigh. Select the configured bass effects with one pedal press, then pluck.

To return, stop plucking, support and turn the instrument upright, retrieve the bow, select the cello effect state and resume bowing. The support must permit the handoff; that has not been tested. There is a pause in playing during the movement. The endpin must already be stowed before this proposed live change.

An ordinary floor-supported seated cello setup remains possible, but turning it sideways requires manually stowing the endpin first. That is a different, slower setup; it does not demonstrate the requested quick switch.

## What this changes about the project

The illustrations make a remaining design problem explicit: the earlier wing layout alone did not establish leg support and stable balance in both poses. The next work is fitting actual knee/thigh contacts and a stable weight-bearing support to a seated mock-up, then resolving the metal-to-spine attachment. No new fit, load, bow clearance or switching-time result is claimed here.
