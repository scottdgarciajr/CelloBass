# Design decisions — r0.2

1. **Preserve the musical core.** Link original evaluated meshes in all four scenes. Live cello and bass are related only by a global -90 degree rotation about Y, around the provisional point (0,-40,280) mm. This is a kinematic reference, not an installed bearing.
2. **Freeze the live wing pose.** Both live states have zero wing-fold angle. A regression recovers every bass vertex to the cello frame and checks agreement within 0.001 mm. Neck, bridge and string-bearing components never articulate.
3. **Separate travel folding.** Two rear-mounted wing envelopes pivot about Y at (+/-55,-70,150) mm. They fold 90 degrees upward alongside the spine only offstage. The original fixed chest rest remains, so it limits packed width even after the wings fold.
4. **Do not call envelope parts production parts.** The wings have no integrated hinge lugs, metal mounts, buffers, padding or proven ergonomic surface. Their STEP lives outside the printable coupon folder and is marked ENVELOPE ONLY. The hinge coupon is an isolated mechanism with a different local coordinate system; it has not been integrated into the wing/core.
5. **Separate load paths.** The proposed rear aluminum rail and metal anchor coordinates bypass the wings. A positive metal-to-rod connection has NOT been designed or verified. Original printed joint screws alone are not accepted as demonstrated harness load paths. No printed swivel, saddle or anchor coupon is rated to support the instrument.
6. **Reject an unsupported free swivel as a completed solution.** It permits uncontrolled roll and does not establish upright bowing stability. The indexed mock disk requires pin repositioning and therefore is explicitly NOT a live-switch mechanism. It is a bench tool to compare poses before choosing a continuously attached harness.
7. **Use a two-point body harness as an unproven candidate.** Lower and upper rail anchor proposals are (-40,-50,180) and (-40,-50,460) mm. Independent branches must provide a restoring couple and torso contact without a change of attachment. Neither strap tension nor support stability has been solved for a human. The line routes in Blender are schematic, not rated straps.
8. **Pre-stow the endpin.** A 450 mm tube envelope is modeled behind the core, from Z=-160 to 290 mm. Diameter is documented; 1 mm wall and stow location are assumed. There is no proven captive deployment mechanism. Static seated endpin use remains a separate configuration requiring manual stow before rotation.
9. **Keep new material behind the bow side.** The new wing envelopes remain at Y=-82..-70 mm while the bridge front reaches Y=33.213 mm. Conservative wing/core sweep separation passes, but this does not establish bow stroke, strap, cable or clothing clearance.
10. **Keep DSP offboard.** One footswitch toggles a configured bass effects chain. Pitch shifting -8 semitones retains fifth intervals: the open strings become approximately E1-B1-F#2-C#3, not standard bass fourths. No processor has been purchased, configured or acoustically tested.
11. **Hold Gate D.** Production interfaces, full-instrument STLs, a complete solid STEP, tension testing and an achieved live-switch time are intentionally not released. The brief requires support feasibility before building the full instrument. Gate B remains open pending a supported dummy test and structural interface design.

## Practical fallback

Until a stable rotating harness passes, keep the instrument upright and switch from bowing to fingerstyle with the offboard effects footswitch. This preserves the same string geometry and avoids an unsupported sideways transition. It does not satisfy the requested horizontal-bass pose; it is a usable musical fallback, not a passed Gate B.
